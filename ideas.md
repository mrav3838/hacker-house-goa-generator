# Hacker House Goa — Design Direction

## Three possible directions

### Theme Name: Monsoon Signal
Very dark, electric, and atmospheric: a midnight Goa sky with acid signal colors and cybernetic UI cues. It would feel like a late-night hack sprint by the beach.
**Probability:** 0.03

### Theme Name: Portuguese Postcard
A sun-washed editorial system inspired by old Goa architecture, hand-painted signs, and analog travel ephemera. It would feel warm, crafted, and collectible.
**Probability:** 0.06

### Theme Name: Sunset Frequency
A kinetic sunset identity built from coral skies, lagoon greens, hot pink accents, grain, palms, and radio-wave motifs. It would feel celebratory, social, and unmistakably Goa without looking like a generic event page.
**Probability:** 0.02

## Chosen direction: Sunset Frequency

### Design Movement
Contemporary tropical brutalism with 1970s Indian travel-poster energy, softened by editorial typography and a digital collectible sensibility.

### Core Principles
1. **Signal over sameness:** Every surface should feel like a broadcast from a real place, using strong labels, signal marks, and a slightly off-grid composition.
2. **Tropical voltage:** Pair lagoon depths and inky plum with sun-faded apricot, mango, and hot coral so the interface feels warm first and technical second.
3. **Collectible utility:** The generator should behave like a small identity studio, not a form. Every input visibly changes a useful artifact.
4. **Playful precision:** Expressive details are welcome, but spacing, contrast, keyboard access, and hierarchy remain rigorous.

### Color Philosophy
The base is a deep lagoon ink that gives the sunset colors room to glow without resorting to generic neon. Apricot and mango indicate warmth and momentum; coral marks social actions and creative energy; seafoam is reserved for system confirmations and the Solana signal. The ownable brand color is **Sunset Guava** (#FF6B5E), a coral-red that feels like the last five minutes of light over Anjuna.

### Layout Paradigm
Use an asymmetric studio desk: a narrow editorial rail and status strip on the left, a large live artifact stage on the right, with a bottom dock for presets and export. On mobile, the rail becomes a horizontal context strip and the live artifact stays visually dominant above the controls.

### Signature Elements
- A tiny sun-and-signal mark that repeats in the logo, status rail, and export stamp.
- Torn-paper / poster-edge clipping around the live identity card, paired with fine technical registration marks.
- A “Goa frequency” ticker that moves slowly and carries date, place, and build-state metadata.

### Interaction Philosophy
Controls should feel like tuning a broadcast: immediate, tactile, and legible. Inputs update the artifact as the user types. Presets are one-click scene changes. Hover states reveal a little more context, while buttons use confident press feedback rather than glossy effects.

### Animation
Entrance motion is a restrained poster assembly: headline and stage rise 16px with opacity, control groups stagger by 45ms, and the artifact gets a subtle 1.5-degree settling rotation. Hover motion is limited to transform and color. The frequency ticker drifts continuously at low speed. Respect reduced motion by disabling ambient drift and keeping state changes instant.

### Typography System
Display: **Bebas Neue** for event-scale headlines and compact labels. Editorial accent: **DM Serif Display Italic** for Goa / place-led moments. UI and body: **Space Grotesk** for clarity and a crafted technical feel. Use uppercase labels with generous tracking, short line lengths, and a clear 4-level hierarchy.

### Brand Essence
A personal signal generator for builders heading to Hacker House Goa — made for people who want their identity to travel before they do. Personality: **sunny, inventive, magnetic**.

### Brand Voice
Headlines are compact and declarative. CTAs sound like invitations into a scene, not generic software commands. Microcopy is helpful, a little cheeky, and never over-explained.

Example lines:
- “Make your signal visible.”
- “Tune your card. Then take it to Goa.”

### Wordmark & Logo
Use a custom stacked wordmark: a small two-line “HH” monogram shaped like a setting sun over a wave, followed by HACKER HOUSE in condensed capitals and Goa in a handwritten serif italic. The symbol must work independently as the favicon and as a stamp on exported cards.

### Signature Brand Color
**Sunset Guava — #FF6B5E**

## Style Decisions
- Keep the visual language warm, editorial, and tropical; avoid purple gradients and generic dashboard cards.
- Favor charcoal-lagoon surfaces, thin warm borders, and asymmetry over uniform rounded containers.
- Treat the live identity artifact as the hero, not a secondary preview.
